The Old Testament Unveiled · A Companion Chart

A Biblical Timeline

From Adam to the fall of the temple, and after: every major event with its passage, and the stone that names it

"To everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven."

Gold marker: a biblical event, with its passage. Scarlet marker: an entry with evidence outside the Bible, the inscription, tablet, chronicle, or excavation that names the same people or events, linked in the chips beneath it. Dates before Abraham are given in the story's own count of years (Anno Mundi), not as calendar dates; from Abraham onward, dates are BC and AD by the traditional biblical reckoning, with the main scholarly alternative noted where it differs.

The bar is to scale from Abraham to the present. The list below is not: the Bible spends more pages on one century of kings than on a thousand years of patriarchs, and so does this chart.

Before Abraham: the Story's Own Count

Genesis 5 and 11 give 1,656 years from creation to the flood and 2,008 to Abraham's birth. These are the text's internal reckoning, shown here without a calendar date.

AD 363

Julian the Apostate tries to rebuild the temple; fire drives the workers off

Matthew 24:2; Luke 21:6 (foretold)

The last pagan emperor, wanting to refute Jesus's prophecy and win the Jews against the church, ordered the temple rebuilt at imperial expense. The pagan historian Ammianus Marcellinus, Julian's own officer and admirer, records the result: 'fearful balls of fire, breaking out near the foundations, continued their attacks, till the workmen, after repeated scorchings, could approach no more, and he gave up the attempt' (Res Gestae 23.1.3). Christian writers (Gregory of Nazianzus, Chrysostom, Rufinus, Sozomen, Theodoret) add an earthquake and crosses appearing on garments. Modern historians accept the attempt and its fiery abandonment as fact, and most connect it to the great Galilee earthquake of 19 May 363, documented in destruction layers from Petra to Sepphoris, with gas or oil pockets in the vaults beneath the Temple Mount ignited by it. Whether that is a natural explanation or the mechanism of a judgment is the one point on which the pagan witness and the Christian ones do not need to agree; Julian was dead by June, and no one has tried since.
