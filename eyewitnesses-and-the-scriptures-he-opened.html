What this is

The title is the two series' own description of themselves. The New Testament's writers claimed to be "eyewitnesses of his majesty" (2 Peter 1:16) and said why they wrote: "these are written so that you may believe" (John 20:31). The Old Testament is what Jesus, on the road to Emmaus, "opened to them" (Luke 24:32), "everything written about me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms." So the first series is written in the voice of the authors, and the second in the words Jesus used for Israel's Scriptures.

This is a free, complete, book-by-book study of the entire Bible, written in plain English for the thoughtful reader, the honest skeptic, and the believer who has never been shown how the two Testaments fit. It comes in two series, meant to be read in order. The first, The Writers of the New Testament, walks all twenty-seven books one author at a time, because that is how they were written: by a tax collector, a physician, an interpreter, a beloved disciple, a converted persecutor, a mended fisherman, two skeptical brothers, and one majestic anonymous. The second, The Old Testament Unveiled, walks the whole Hebrew Bible in the order Jesus himself named it, "the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms," reading Israel's Scriptures the way the apostles read them after the resurrection, with the synagogue's own reading stated beside theirs at every contested page. Sixteen volumes, an orientation to what a Jewish Bible actually is, a companion on the books between the Testaments, and two sources pages: roughly half a million words, all of it here, none of it behind a paywall.

What makes it different

Author by author, shelf by shelf

Not a verse-by-verse commentary and not a survey. Each New Testament writer gets his own volume, with who he was, when and to whom he wrote, and how his voice differs from the others. Each Old Testament shelf gets its own volume in the Hebrew order, ending where the Hebrew Bible ends: "let him go up."

Evidence first, and every claim sourced

Manuscripts, the earliest church witnesses, the non-Christian sources, the inscriptions and the stones. Two sources pages link every claim to a public copy: the British Museum's own record of the Taylor Prism, the Louvre's Mesha Stele, the Israel Museum's Isaiah Scroll, Josephus, the church fathers, and the canon lists.

The synagogue at the table

At every contested prophecy (Isaiah 7:14, Isaiah 53, Psalm 110, Daniel 7, and their kin) a panel states the classical Jewish reading first and at full strength, from Rashi, the Targums, and the Talmud, linked to Sefaria and Chabad, before the series answers. A reading you have never heard at full strength is a reading you have not yet answered.

Sealed in the New Testament

Nearly two hundred scarlet-and-gold badges mark the places where an apostle cites, alludes to, or builds an argument on the Old Testament passage in hand, so the reader watches the Old Testament's New Testament career happen in real time, from Genesis 3:15 to the Chronicler's last word.

Context most readers were never given

What a Tanakh is, why the Christian Old Testament is ordered differently, what the apostles' Greek Bible looked like, which other Jewish books they read and quoted without calling Scripture, and what a Jewish reader studies today. The orientation and the companion volume supply what seminary courses assume and Sunday schools skip.

Plain terms, and honest edges

Every section closes with a gold "In plain terms" box the reader can carry. Disputed texts are stated at their strongest on every side. The series names its own confessional accent rather than smuggling it, welcomes Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant readers alike, and invites corrections in every footer.

How to read it

  1. Start with the New Testament series, in its eight volumes. It is the shorter of the two, and it teaches the method: real authors, real evidence, and a Bible you can check.
  2. Read the orientation, "Before You Begin: What the Tanakh Is." Twenty minutes, and it explains why the Old Testament series is arranged as it is.
  3. Walk the Old Testament, Volume One through Volume Eight, in order. Each volume stands alone, but the walk was built to be taken in sequence.
  4. Then the companion volume, "The Books Between the Testaments," for the room in which the apostles opened the Scriptures.
  5. Check the work. Both sources pages link every historical and rabbinic claim to a public copy. Corrections are welcome at the address in every footer.

About the author

H. W. Mahaffey lives in the Pacific Northwest with his six children, where he manages hydroelectric operations on the Columbia River as a NERC-certified power grid operator. Alongside that career he has pursued a disciplined independent study of the etymology of ancient languages and of classical philosophy, an inquiry that led him, by way of the vocabulary of creation in the Hebrew and Greek Scriptures, into modern information theory and cosmology. To test whether the ancient "logic" of creation could be mapped onto the physical cosmos, he taught himself theoretical cosmology and higher mathematics, learning to run cosmological modeling software and to code the stress-tests himself, and published the results with an open invitation to critique. He writes these Bible studies for the reader he once was: curious, skeptical, and unwilling to take anyone's word for it without seeing the sources.

Also by the author: (IAM) Informational Actualization Model: The Synthesis: How Modern Physics, Classical Philosophy, and Christian Theology Converge on a Coherent Vision of Reality (hardcover, paperback, and eBook), a book-length argument that the physical sciences, the classical philosophical tradition, and Christian theology converge rather than compete.
