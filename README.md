# Mahaffey: The Incredible Origin of the Surname and the Bloodline

*Mac Gille Chathbhaidh — the sons of the servant of Saint Cathbad*

---

A fully sourced history of the **Mahaffey** surname, tracing it to the Gaelic **Mac Gille Chathbhaidh**, "son of the servant of Saint Cathbad," and to the chapel lands of **Kellechaffe** at **Craigcaffie** on **Loch Ryan** in Galloway, Scotland.

Thirty-seven cited sources. Every claim is marked as documented, tradition, legend, or unavailable.

## What the paper establishes

- **The etymology.** George F. Black's *The Surnames of Scotland* (p. 461) derives Machaffie / Mahaffey from **Mac Gille Chathbhaidh**, "son of the servant of S. Cathbad," and records the same saint's name in the place name Craig Caffie, parish of Inch, Wigtownshire.
- **The land.** The chapel lands appear as **Kellechaffe** in a charter of Robert the Bruce, and as **Killichaffie in 1329** in the University of Glasgow's survey of saints in Scottish place-names. The ground carried the family's name in writing more than two centuries before any individual bearing it does.
- **The saint.** Identified as **Cathub mac Fergusa** — Cathub, son of Fergus — a bishop commemorated on **6 April** in the Martyrologies of Tallaght and Donegal, his death recorded about 555.
- **The first man on record.** **John McCaffe, king's messenger, 1540**, in the Accounts of the Lord High Treasurer of Scotland.
- **Ulster and America.** First recorded in Ireland as **MacHaffy in the County Armagh hearth money rolls of 1664**; in Pennsylvania by 1753; on Revolutionary muster rolls within a generation.
- **Mahaffey is not Clan Macfie.** Black keeps the names in separate entries, and surname DNA projects find the sound-alike groups to be genetically distinct families. The paper explains why the error persists and why Mahaffey nonetheless sits on the Macfie roll of associated names.
